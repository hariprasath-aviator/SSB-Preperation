UNIFIED PAYMENT INTERFACE

Introduction:

Unified Payment Interface (UPI) is the real time payment system that enables instant money transfer between bank accounts through a mobile phone. It was developed by the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) and regulated by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) . It is one of the major developments of the Digital India initiative and an important component of the digital public infrastructure.

What is UPI?

UPI enables

  • Person to Person (P2P).
  • Person to Merchant (P2M).
A user can make payments using
  1. Mobile Number.
  2. UPI ID or Virtual Payment Address (VPA).
  3. QR Code.
Eliminating the need to share sensitive banking information

Why was UPI introduced? - History

Before UPI, India had already developed electronic payment systems.

  • RTGS- 2004 :

    Real-Time Gross Settlement enabled real-time bank-to-bank fund transfers, mainly suited for high-value transactions.

  • IMPS - 2010 :

    Immediate Payment Service enabled instant fund transfers but generally required details such as account numbers and IFSC codes.

The problem is that India needed a payment system that was:

Simple + Instant + Interoperable + Accessible + Scalable.

At the same time, India was building the JAM Trinity:

  1. Jan Dhan Yojana (2014) - Financial inclusion through bank accounts.
  2. Aadhaar (2009) - Digital identity through biometric authentication.
  3. Mobile (2010s) - Connectivity through mobile phones.

That's when UPI was launched in 2016. At first the adoption was gradual. But it increased rapidly with the expansion of mobile connectivity, affordable internet and digital payment systems.

The 2019 pandemic further accelerated the adoption as it highlighted the need for contactless payments.

Advantages:

  • Fast: Instant 24x7 payments.
  • Convenient: Payments through mobile/QR code.
  • Convenient: Payments through mobile/QR code.
  • Transparent: Creates digital transaction records
  • Supports Digital India: Major component of India's DPI.
  • Global soft power: Demonstrates India's technological capability.
  • Financial inclusion: Helps rural citizens and small merchants.

Disadvantages:

  • Cybersecurity - Increasing digital transactions also increase exposure to:
    1. Phishing
    2. QR-code fraud
    3. Fake payment requests
    4. Social engineering
    5. Screen-sharing scams
  • Digital Divide - People Without :
    1. Smartphones.
    2. Internet Connectivity.
    3. Digital literacy.
  • Infrastructure Pressure
    1. Banks.
    2. Payment servers.
    3. Core Banking Systems.

Recent Developments - 2026:

  • In August 2026 : 24.51 billion transactions which means nearly 80 cr per day.
  • Record July 2026: Around 2,366 crore transactions worth nearly ₹29.88 lakh crore.
  • MDR (Merchant Discount Rate) is a fee charged to merchants for processing digital credit, debit, or QR code payments.It is collected to sustain and secure the payment infrastructure by splitting the revenue among the bank, card network, and payment gateway.
  • The Favara-UPI cross-border payment corridor links the Maldives' Favara instant payment system with India's Unified Payments Interface (UPI).It enables real-time money transfers from the Maldives in Maldivian Rufiyaa to be instantly credited as Indian Rupees into UPI-enabled accounts in India.
